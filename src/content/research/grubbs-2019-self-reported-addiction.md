## Summary

Using a nationally representative sample of US adults, Grubbs, Kraus, and Perry (2019)
examined who reports feeling "addicted" to pornography and what predicts that feeling.

## Key findings

Religiousness and moral disapproval were stronger predictors of self-reported addiction
than the amount of pornography a person actually used. This supports the moral
incongruence account: people are more likely to feel "addicted" when their use conflicts
with their values.

## Why it matters

This study is frequently cited in the [behavioural addiction debate](/topics/behavioral-addiction-debate/)
because it suggests that self-identification as "addicted" is not a simple reflection of
heavy use. It informs how we read community claims about [PPU](/topics/ppu/).
