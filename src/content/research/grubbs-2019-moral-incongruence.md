## Summary

Grubbs, Perry, Wilt, and Reid (2019) integrated existing research and conducted a
systematic review and meta-analysis of the relationship between moral incongruence and
self-reported problems with pornography. Moral incongruence refers to the distress people
feel when their behaviour conflicts with their moral or religious values.

## Main finding

The authors found that moral incongruence is a major — and frequently the strongest —
predictor of whether a person labels themselves as "addicted" to pornography. This label
is often driven more by the moral conflict than by how much pornography the person
actually views.

## Why it matters

This work is central to understanding [how PPU is defined](/topics/ppu-definition/) and
why the [behavioural addiction debate](/topics/behavioral-addiction-debate/) is not only
about brain science but also about values and self-perception. It does not, on its own,
settle whether heavy use is harmful.
