## Summary

Brand and colleagues (2016) proposed the I-PACE model (Interaction of
Person-Affect-Cognition-Execution) to explain how specific Internet-use disorders develop
and persist. It is not itself a study but a widely used theoretical framework.

## How the model works

The model describes how a person's predispositions interact with affective and cognitive
responses to cues, and with reduced executive control, to drive compulsive patterns of
use. It is frequently applied to problematic pornography use, linking
[cue reactivity](/topics/cue-reactivity/), [emotion regulation](/topics/emotion-regulation/),
and [self-regulation](/topics/self-regulation/).

## Why it matters

The I-PACE model provides a coherent, testable account — but it remains a model, not
established fact. It appears here because it is commonly cited when explaining PPU.
