position_a: "Reducing or stopping pornography use leads to genuine improvements in mood, confidence, and relationships."
evidence_a:
  - "Many individuals report improvements after stopping; some studies find associations between problematic use and poorer mental health."
limitations_a:
  - "Self-reports are susceptible to expectation effects."
  - "People who stop often make many simultaneous changes (sleep, exercise, socializing)."
position_b: "Reported improvements largely reflect reduced shame and moral incongruence, and improvements in co-occurring lifestyle factors, rather than abstinence per se."
evidence_b:
  - "Studies find self-perceived problems with pornography are strongly tied to moral incongruence."
limitations_b:
  - "This research does not rule out genuine benefits of reduced use for some."
well_established:
  - "Problematic use is associated with distress; reducing distress is a legitimate goal."
  - "Behavior change is usually part of a broader pattern of life changes."
remains_uncertain:
  - "How much of reported improvement is directly caused by abstinence"
community_experience: "Improvement accounts are common and consistent; they are often accompanied by broader lifestyle changes."
current_assessment: "People's reported improvements are real experiences. Research cannot yet separate how much comes from abstinence itself versus reduced shame, expectation, and lifestyle changes. Both pathways are legitimate reasons to continue."
