## What impulse control disorders are

Impulse control disorders are conditions characterized by difficulty resisting an impulse
or temptation to act in a way that is harmful to oneself or others. The ICD-11 places
[CSBD](/topics/csbd/) in this chapter.

## Why CSBD sits here

The placement reflects a deliberate decision not to classify CSBD as an addiction — a
decision that remains contested. It is a classification choice, not proof that the
underlying mechanism is best described as impaired impulse control (indeed, some research
points to compulsivity over impulsivity; see [Bőthe et al. (2019)](/research/bothe-2019-impulsivity/)).

## Practical relevance

For most people, the diagnostic label matters less than the practical question: does the
behaviour cause distress or impairment? Strategies that delay or interrupt impulsive
action remain useful regardless. See [impulse control](/topics/impulse-control/) and the
[behavioural addiction debate](/topics/behavioral-addiction-debate/).
