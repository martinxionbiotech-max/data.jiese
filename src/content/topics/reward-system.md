## What the reward system is

The reward system is a network of brain regions — centrally the ventral striatum and
midbrain dopamine neurons — involved in motivation, pleasure, learning, and the
anticipation of rewards. It is not a single "pleasure center" but a distributed system.

## What the research shows

Some studies, such as [Gola et al. (2017)](/research/gola-2017-fmri/) and
[Voon et al. (2014)](/research/voon-2014-cue-reactivity/), report heightened reward-system
activity — especially in anticipation of sexual cues — in people with compulsive sexual
behaviour. These findings are suggestive but come from small, cross-sectional samples and
are not fully consistent (see [Prause et al. (2015)](/research/prause-2015-lpp/)).

## What it does and does not mean

Altered reward activity does not by itself prove addiction, harm, or that any change is
permanent. It does support the idea that anticipation and motivation are partly automatic,
which can reduce self-blame. See [dopamine](/topics/dopamine/) and
[neuroplasticity](/topics/neuroplasticity/).
