## The distinction

An **urge** is a felt impulse or pressure to act, often fleeting and situation-bound. A
**craving** is usually described as more intense, enduring, and consciously elaborated.
The two terms are related but not identical.

## In research and in the community

Addiction research sometimes treats craving as a stronger form of urge with distinct
neural correlates. In the PPU field the distinction is used loosely. Community members
use the terms interchangeably but often reserve "craving" for the harder-to-resist
experiences.

## Practical relevance

A useful self-monitoring practice is to distinguish a passing urge from a sustained
craving: most urges rise and fall on their own. Tracking intensity and duration is a
common technique. See [craving](/topics/craving/) and [cue reactivity](/topics/cue-reactivity/).
